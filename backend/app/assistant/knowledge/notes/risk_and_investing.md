# Risk management and investing basics

## Position sizing
Decide how much you can lose on one trade before entering, typically 1–2% of trading capital. Position size equals that rupee risk divided by the distance from entry to stop. This keeps a losing streak survivable: ten 2% losses in a row cost about 18%, while ten 10% losses cost about 65%.

## Stop-loss
A stop-loss is the price at which you accept being wrong and exit. Place it beyond a meaningful level (below support for a long position) rather than at an arbitrary percentage, and size the position so that hitting it costs no more than your planned risk. StockSage's rule R13 sells a holding 8% below its average buy price by default.

## Reward to risk
Compare the potential gain to your target with the loss to your stop. A ratio of at least 1.5 to 2 means you can be wrong more often than right and still make money.

## Diversification
Spread money across stocks, sectors and asset classes so that one bad event does not sink the portfolio. StockSage caps any one sector at 35% of invested equity (rule R9) and keeps allocations across Nifty 50, Next 100, small caps and gold/silver ETFs.

## Investing versus trading
Investing means owning good businesses for years and riding out volatility; returns come from business growth. Trading means taking shorter positions based on price behaviour; returns depend on timing and discipline, and costs and taxes matter more. Most retail F&O traders lose money: SEBI studies found that around 9 out of 10 individual F&O traders made net losses in recent years.

## SIP and rupee-cost averaging
A systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, buying more units when prices are low and fewer when high. It removes the need to time the market and suits long-term investors.

## Behavioural mistakes
Common mistakes: averaging down on losing trades without a reason, moving stop-losses further away, chasing stocks after big rises, selling winners too early while holding losers, overtrading, and acting on tips or social media hype.

## Asking about the future
No tool can predict tomorrow's price. Analysis can describe the current trend, valuation, sentiment and risk, and estimate ranges (such as the option market's expected move), but any forecast is a probability, not a certainty. StockSage is a research tool, not investment advice.
