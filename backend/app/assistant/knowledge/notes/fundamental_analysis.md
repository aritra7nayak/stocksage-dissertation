# Fundamental analysis: ratios and what they mean

## P/E ratio (price to earnings)
P/E is the share price divided by earnings per share: how many rupees investors pay for one rupee of yearly profit. A lower P/E can mean cheap or can mean the market expects profits to fall; a high P/E means investors expect growth. Compare P/E with the company's own history and its sector, not across sectors: IT and FMCG companies usually trade at higher P/Es than metals or PSU banks. The Nifty 50 has historically traded roughly between 18 and 25 times trailing earnings.

## P/B ratio (price to book)
Price divided by book value (net assets) per share. Most useful for banks and financial companies, whose assets are mostly financial. A P/B below 1 means the market values the company below its accounting net worth, which can signal value or doubts about asset quality.

## ROE (return on equity)
Net profit divided by shareholders' equity: how efficiently the company turns shareholders' money into profit. Above 15% is generally good, above 20% excellent. High ROE built on high debt is riskier than high ROE with little debt.

## Debt to equity
Total debt divided by equity. Below 0.5 is conservative, above 1 is leveraged for most non-financial companies. Banks and NBFCs are naturally highly leveraged, so judge them on capital adequacy and asset quality instead.

## Revenue growth and profit margin
Revenue growth shows whether the business is expanding; consistent double-digit growth is strong. Profit margin (net profit divided by revenue) shows pricing power and efficiency; compare margins within a sector, since retailers and refiners run thin margins while software and branded consumer goods run wide ones.

## Dividend yield
Annual dividend per share divided by price. High yields can be attractive but can also reflect a falling share price or an unsustainable payout. PSU companies in India often have high yields.

## EPS and earnings results
Earnings per share is net profit divided by the number of shares. Quarterly results are judged against expectations: a 10% profit rise can still send a stock down if analysts expected 20%. Watch revenue, operating margin (EBITDA margin), profit, and management guidance.

## Banking-specific metrics
For banks: NII (net interest income), NIM (net interest margin, typically 3–4% for Indian private banks), gross and net NPA (non-performing assets; lower is better), CASA ratio (share of cheap current and savings deposits; higher is better) and capital adequacy ratio.

## Valuation in context
No ratio works alone. A good business at a fair price combines sustainable growth, high ROE, low debt and a valuation that does not already assume perfection. StockSage's fundamental score weighs P/E 20%, ROE 20%, debt/equity 15%, revenue growth 15%, profit margin 15%, P/B 10% and dividend yield 5%, scoring each 0–10 with a reason; missing data scores a neutral 5.

## Fundamental versus technical
Fundamentals answer "is this a good business at a fair price?" and matter over months and years. Technicals answer "what is the price doing now?" and matter over days and weeks. Many investors use fundamentals to choose what to own and technicals to choose when.
