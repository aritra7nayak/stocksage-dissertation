# The Indian stock market: structure, rules and terms

Rules marked "as of" change over time; always confirm current values with NSE, SEBI or the Income Tax Department before acting.

## Exchanges and indices
India's two main exchanges are the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE (formerly the Bombay Stock Exchange). The Nifty 50 (NSE) and the Sensex (BSE, 30 stocks) are the headline indices. Other key NSE indices include Nifty Bank (BANKNIFTY), Nifty Financial Services (FINNIFTY), Nifty Midcap Select (MIDCPNIFTY), Nifty Next 50, and sector indices such as Nifty IT, Nifty Pharma and Nifty Metal.

## Trading hours
The normal equity session runs from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm IST, Monday to Friday, with a pre-open session from 9:00 to 9:15 am. The market is closed on exchange holidays, which NSE publishes each year. Special sessions such as Muhurat trading on Diwali are announced separately.

## Settlement
Indian equities settle on T+1 (the trade day plus one working day) since 2023, so shares bought today reach your demat account the next working day.

## Regulators and participants
SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) regulates markets, brokers, mutual funds and listed companies. RBI (Reserve Bank of India) sets monetary policy (the repo rate) and regulates banks and NBFCs. FIIs or FPIs are foreign institutional or portfolio investors; DIIs are domestic institutions such as mutual funds, insurers and pension funds. Their daily net buying or selling (FII/DII flows) is closely watched: heavy FII selling often weighs on the market, while strong DII buying can cushion it.

## Futures and options (F&O)
Index and stock derivatives trade on NSE in fixed lot sizes that NSE revises periodically. As of 2025–2026, after SEBI's derivatives reforms, NSE allows one weekly expiry per exchange (the Nifty 50 weekly), monthly contracts for other indices and stocks expire on the last Tuesday of the month, and index lot sizes were raised. Exact lot sizes and expiry days change; the StockSage options page always shows the live lot size and expiries.

## Price bands and circuit breakers
Individual stocks have price bands (for example 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%) beyond which they cannot trade that day; F&O stocks have dynamic bands instead. Market-wide circuit breakers halt trading if the Nifty or Sensex falls 10%, 15% or 20% in a day.

## India VIX
India VIX measures the market's expected volatility over the next 30 days, derived from Nifty option prices. Readings around 11–15 are calm, above 20 nervous, above 25–30 fearful. A rising VIX makes options more expensive.

## Taxes on equity (as of the July 2024 Union Budget)
Short-term capital gains on listed equity held 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Long-term gains (held more than 12 months) above ₹1.25 lakh a year are taxed at 12.5%. Gains from futures and options are treated as business income and taxed at your slab rate. Securities Transaction Tax (STT) applies to equity delivery, intraday and F&O trades at different rates; STT on options is charged on the premium when selling. Dividends are taxed at your slab rate. Tax rules change with budgets; verify current rates.

## Types of orders
A market order executes at the best available price immediately; a limit order executes only at your price or better. A stop-loss order becomes active when a trigger price is hit. For options, limit orders are strongly preferred because spreads can be wide.

## Delivery, intraday and margin
Delivery (CNC) trades keep shares in your demat account. Intraday (MIS) positions must be closed the same day. Leverage for intraday and F&O is set by SEBI's margin rules; F&O requires upfront margin (SPAN plus exposure).

## Corporate actions
Dividends, bonus issues (free extra shares), stock splits (more shares at a proportionally lower price), rights issues and buybacks change share counts or pay cash. Prices adjust on the ex-date; a split or bonus does not change the value of your holding by itself.

## Key market terms
Market capitalisation is share price times shares outstanding; large caps are roughly the top 100 companies, mid caps the next 150, and small caps the rest (SEBI definitions). An IPO is a company's first public share sale; listing gains are the difference between the issue price and the first-day price. Grey market premium (GMP) is an unofficial pre-listing price indicator and is not reliable.
